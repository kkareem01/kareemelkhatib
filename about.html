Background

My Story

Electrical engineering, AI, and a family business — how the three fit together.

I’m Kareem Elkhatib, an electrical engineering student at the University of Georgia, class of 2028, based in Atlanta. My days split between two worlds that turn out to be the same world: the physics underneath modern hardware, and the AI software being built on top of it.

The engineering

At UGA I study circuits, signals, and the fundamentals that make electronics work — and I like proving things on a bench, not just on paper. A recent project: an active low-pass filter I assembled on a breadboard, deriving its transfer function and cutoff frequency from the circuit model, then confirming the frequency response with a network analyzer.

My favorite build so far came from a real problem. I film weddings and events around Atlanta, and my camera would die in minutes on its stock battery. So I engineered an external power system — matching the OEM voltage requirements with a dummy-battery and V-mount setup — that took runtime up roughly twenty-fold and made full-day shoots possible. Understand how the thing works, then build it properly.

The business

Business runs in the family. At Suitstation, our menswear business in Georgia, I handle growth — sales campaigns and funnels, plus the inventory and reorder systems behind them. I’ve also worked as a sales manager for a startup mentorship program, closing $20,000 in revenue running a high-ticket sales framework.

Now

Today those threads meet in one place: building AI-powered software and systems for the electrical engineering and business industries — agents, automations, and tools that handle real work end-to-end.

Off the clock, you’ll find me behind a camera or on a mic — filming around Atlanta and public speaking at UGA.
